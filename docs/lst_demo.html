Surface vs air temperature — the August trend

Comparing the same calendar month across years removes seasonal bias. The satellite land-surface temperature (LST) runs several degrees above ERA5 2 m air temperature — that gap is the daytime forest canopy heating in the sun.

Read as surface, not air. LST is the clear-sky, ~10:30 overpass skin temperature — physically warmer than the 24-hour mean air temperature a weather station reports. The ~5 °C August surplus is that surface-minus-air signal.

How each frame is made

Median of many looks

For each month, every cloud-filtered satellite scene is stacked and reduced pixel-by-pixel with a median. A single bright cloud that slipped the mask is outvoted rather than averaged in — the result is a clean composite, not one lucky image.

Each frame is annotated with n, the number of scenes it combined.

Honest gaps

A month with no cloud-free thermal view is left out rather than faked. Landsat's 16-day revisit means a persistently cloudy stretch — e.g. August 2021, three overpasses, all 100 % cloud over the AOI — simply has no frame.

Per-frame cloud fraction and mean temperature are logged to a queryable database.

What's in the pipeline

  • Products: land-surface temperature (LST), NDVI, true-colour, colour-infrared, plus vegetation & moisture indices.
  • Sensors: Sentinel-2 (10 m), Landsat 8/9 (30 m optical, 100 m thermal), MODIS.
  • Output: 4K MP4 + GIF, per-month frames, and a metadata DB (scene count, AOI cloud, avg temperature).
  • Rigour: fixed viz scale, metric-CRS rendering, cloud masking inside the AOI, native-resolution honesty.
